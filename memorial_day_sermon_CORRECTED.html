MOUNTAIN VIEW COMMUNITY CHURCH

Greater Love Has No One

*Memorial Day 2026 | John 15:13*

Opening Prayer

*"Heavenly Father, we gather today to remember those who gave everything. We honor the fallen. We grieve with those who mourn. And we ask that You give us the courage to never forget their sacrifice. Speak through me today. Let Your words be mine. In Jesus' name, Amen."*

Introduction

Happy Memorial Day.

Before I go any further - Let's start with, no matter where we journey today, don't feel guilty for your hotdogs and hamburgers and beer today.

Don't feel guilty for enjoying your day with your family.

The fallen would not be happy if you moped around feeling guilty.

They didn't die so any of us could sit in the dark and be miserable.

They died so you could LIVE FREE.

So enjoy your freedom today. Laugh. Celebrate. LIVE.

That's WHY they served. [PAUSE]

But we DO need to remember.

And we need to understand the difference between today and Veterans Day.

Memorial Day = THE FALLEN. The ones who didn't come home.

Veterans Day = THE LIVING. Those who served and came back.

Today - we honor those who gave everything. [PAUSE]

So I brought something with me today. In front of the podium. [Show the memorial plaque]

This is my Memorial Day. Some were soldiers I served with in Ramadi, Iraq, 2005-2006. Some were fellow service members from Korea and other deployments. Some were civilians caught in the crossfire of evil. Some defined a military career.

Every name on this plaque represents a life cut short. A family forever changed. A sacrifice we can never repay.

And today, we remember them and more. [LONG PAUSE]

The weight of a folded flag - measurable in ounces.

Thirteen folds. 2.3 pounds.

But to those left behind, it's heavier than the world. [LONG PAUSE]

POINT 1: Sacrifice and Service

John 15:13

**"Greater love has no one than this: to lay down one's life for one's friends."**

That's what we're honoring today. Love. Sacrifice. Service.

The Purple Heart Inventory

Most people may not know this.

In 1945, we were preparing to invade Japan. The Battle of Okinawa showed us how fierce the Japanese defense would be. We saw what was coming.

We prepared half a million Purple Hearts in order to give out to those wounded and killed (those killed would receive posthumously).

Half a million.

In total, we expected 1.7 to 4 MILLION American casualties. That's how bad we thought it would be.

Those medals are still in our inventory, the same medals today - Korea, Vietnam, Iraq, Afghanistan, the War on Terror.

We've been at war for 70+ years since World War II ended, and we STILL haven't used up all the Purple Hearts we made for an invasion that never happened.

That's how committed America was to victory. We were willing to bleed that much to stop evil. [PAUSE]

No One Hates War More

Something you need to understand:

No one hates war more than those who go and the loved ones left behind.

No one.

I've seen it, lived it, felt it; I know the cost.

War is never taken lightly. Not by the soldier who leaves his family. Not by the wife who kisses him goodbye not knowing if he'll come home. Not by the mother who watches her son deploy. Not by the father who buries his daughter.

We don't glorify war. We do honor sacrifice.

And yet the soldier, marine, airman and sailor do it anyway.

Why?

Because evil exists.

And sometimes, good people have to fight it. [PAUSE]

POINT 2: Fighting for Good

Romans 13:4

**"For the one in authority is God's servant for your good. But if you do wrong, be afraid, for rulers do not bear the sword for no reason. They are God's servants, agents of wrath to bring punishment on the wrongdoer."**

God establishes authority. And sometimes, authority must FIGHT.

Not because we want to. But because evil must be opposed.

The US Military is Built Around Doing Right

We're not perfect. We've made mistakes. I'm not going to stand here and tell you America has never done anything wrong.

But our military is built around protecting the innocent and upholding justice.

A contrast.

The Nazi SS systematically exterminated 6 million Jews, POWs, and civilians. That was their JOB. That's what they were DESIGNED to do.

Our Military? When we found out our soldiers abused prisoners at Abu Ghraib, we PROSECUTED them. We held ourselves accountable. We said: "That's not who we are. That's not what we stand for."

We do our best to be JUST. [PAUSE]

The Restraint of Power

America has the most powerful military in the world.

My opinion, If we wanted to be conquerors, we could.

But look at what we did after World War II:

Germany - We rebuilt it. Marshall Plan. Made them a democracy.

Japan - We rebuilt it. Made them a democracy.

We don't occupy. We liberate. Then we LEAVE.

That's not what empires do. That's what JUST nations do.

The Question We Need to Ask

And this is where I need to be careful. But I'm going to ask it anyway.

Japan attacked us once. Pearl Harbor. December 7, 1941.

And we went full throttle. Total commitment. Total war. Victory. We made half a million Purple Hearts because we were ALL IN.

The Iran Regime (not the people) and its proxies have been killing Americans for decades.

And we've hesitated. And we debate and accuse. Yet we still have men and women sacrificing today. What do our arguments do for them?

I'm not here to tell you what policy should be. I'm not a politician. I don't have all the answers.

But I AM here to ask: What happened to the America that woke up on September 12, 2001?

Do you remember that day?

United. Proud. Ready to defend freedom at any cost. Flags on every porch. We were AMERICANS first. We know freedom that most of the world does not.

And sometimes I wonder - have we forgotten?

I have a connection I'm trying to make - I'll come back to this in the end. [PAUSE]

Evil still exists. And good people still have to be willing to fight it.

I'm not saying we should rush into war. War should always be the last resort.

But I AM saying: when evil shows itself, we can't just look away. [PAUSE]

POINT 3: Jesus the Warrior

The Misunderstanding

Now - you might be questioning my train of thought: "Wait, Matt. Didn't Jesus say to turn the other cheek? Didn't He tell us to love our enemies? How does that fit with what you're saying?"

Good question.

We sometimes think of Jesus as weak. Gentle. Passive.

"Turn the other cheek" sounds like "be a doormat."

But that's NOT what Jesus meant.

"Turn the Other Cheek" - Cultural Context

In Roman culture, a backhand slap to the right cheek was how a superior insulted an inferior.

A master to a slave. A Roman to a Jew.

It wasn't about physical harm. It was about SHAME. It was saying: "You're beneath me. You're nothing."

When Jesus said "turn the other cheek," He wasn't saying "let people abuse you."

He was saying: "Force them to treat you as an EQUAL. Don't let them degrade you. Stand your ground WITHOUT violence."

You see, turning the left cheek meant they had to hit you with an open hand - like an equal - or switch to their left hand, which was socially awkward and humiliating FOR THEM.

That's not weakness. That's DEFIANCE.

That's a WARRIOR refusing to be shamed.

Jesus wasn't telling people to be passive. He was teaching them how to resist oppression with DIGNITY. [PAUSE]

Revelation 19 - Jesus Returns as a Warrior

And if you think Jesus is only gentle and meek, let's look at Revelation 19.

Jesus returns: On a white horse. With a robe dipped in blood. With a sword coming from His mouth. Treading the winepress of God's wrath.

The Lamb and the Lion are the SAME Jesus.

He's gentle when He needs to be. Fierce when He needs to be.

He wept over Jerusalem. And He overturned tables in the temple.

He told Peter to put away his sword. And He told His disciples to buy swords.

Jesus had a warrior's heart.

And He calls us to fight for what's right.

Not with violence for violence's sake. But with courage. With conviction. With a willingness to stand against evil even when it costs us. [LONG PAUSE]

POINT 4: Kayla and the Heart of Sacrifice

Not everyone on that memorial plaque is military.

Kayla Mueller.

Kayla's story. [PAUSE]

She was 26 years old. From Prescott, Arizona. Humanitarian worker.

For a time, before Lindsay, she was mine for a bit. We dated a little. I was her soldier.

She had this heart for people that I've never seen in anyone else. She couldn't sit still when people were suffering. She had to DO something. She had to help. She had explored the world.

So she ended up in Syria. To help refugees who had nothing.

And ISIS took her.

She was held for 18 months. Tortured. Brutalized by evil men.

But she wrote to her family from captivity: "I have a lot of fight left inside of me."

That was Kayla. A warrior's heart.

She never came home.

But in 2019, Delta Force killed al-Baghdadi, her captor. And they named the operation after HER.

Operation Kayla Mueller.

That's what commitment looks like. The United States didn't forget her. Delta didn't forget her.

And I will never forget her. [LONG PAUSE]

Why Choice Matters

Something that connects all of this.

God gave us choice.

Even knowing we would screw it up. Even knowing we'd choose sin. Choose rebellion. Choose ourselves over Him.

He still gave us the choice.

God could have made us robots. Pre-programmed to obey. Incapable of sin.

But He didn't.

Because He wanted REAL relationship. REAL love. And that requires REAL choice.

As Jim talked last week - without choice, it's just obedience. With choice, it's LOVE. [PAUSE]

Jesus could have stayed in heaven. Could have said "They're not worth it."

But He chose to come.

These soldiers could have stayed home. No draft. No one forced them.

But they chose to serve.

Kayla didn't have to go to Syria.

But she chose to go.

Choice is what makes sacrifice MEANINGFUL. [LONG PAUSE]

The Heart of Sacrifice

Kayla gave her life trying to help people who couldn't help themselves.

The men and women on this memorial plaque gave their lives defending people who couldn't defend themselves.

They didn't have to go. They went anyway.

They saw people in need. And they said: "I'll go. I'll stand in the gap. I'll pay the price so others don't have to."

That's the heart of sacrifice. [PAUSE]

And when I think about that - when I think about Kayla going to Syria, knowing the risk - when I think about these soldiers deploying to Ramadi, knowing they might not come home -

I see an echo of something greater.

John 15:13

Jesus said: **"Greater love has no one than this: to lay down one's life for one's friends."**

That's what Kayla did.

That's what these service members did.

And that's what JESUS did. [PAUSE]

Jesus - The Ultimate Sacrifice

Humanity was separated from God.

We were lost. Broken. Enslaved to sin.

And we couldn't save ourselves.

No amount of good works. No amount of religion. No amount of trying harder.

We needed someone to stand in the gap.

We needed someone willing to pay the price we couldn't pay.

And Jesus said: "I'll go."

He left heaven. Became human. Walked into enemy territory.

And He gave everything.

He didn't die because He was weak. He died because He loved us.

**Romans 5:8 - "But God demonstrates his own love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us."**

There are Differences

Kayla's sacrifice and these soldiers', marines', airmen and sailors' sacrifice are NOT the same as Jesus's sacrifice.

Jesus is the ONLY path to God. His death is the ONLY atonement for sin. That's unique. That's unrepeatable.

But the HEART behind it?

That's the same.

"I see people in need. I can't just walk away. I'll go. Even if it costs me everything."

That's love.

That's sacrifice.

That's what we honor today, a heart of sacrifice even if it is generally directed at the military. [PAUSE]

Kayla couldn't save the world. These service members couldn't save the world.

Only Jesus could do that.

But they reflected His heart.

They laid down their lives for others.

And in doing so, they showed us what love looks like. [LONG PAUSE]

Closing: Remember and Live

Now - this sermon is about service members. By intent and design, today is about honoring THEIR sacrifice.

But I want you to see something.

That warrior's heart - the heart that runs toward need when you could walk away - that's not JUST military.

I see it in this church.

I see it in the pantry volunteers who show up every week to feed people.

I see it in the people who serve, who give, who sacrifice their time and energy for others.

Jesus made a sacrifice - universal, unrepeatable, the ONLY path to God.

Service members make a sacrifice - defending freedom, willing to die.

The pantry volunteers make a sacrifice - feeding the hungry, serving the community.

All DIFFERENT sacrifices.

But the SAME HEART.

"I see people in need. I CAN do something. So I WILL. Even if it costs me."

And here's what I want you to understand:

The service members we honor today? They grew up in communities like this.

They learned that warrior's heart HERE.

They saw people running toward need instead of away from it.

They learned sacrifice at HOME. At CHURCH. From YOU.

So when we honor them today, we're also honoring the community that shaped them.

The church that taught them. The families that raised them.

Memorial Day makes MORE SENSE when you see the connection.

That soldier didn't just appear out of nowhere with a heart willing to die for others.

He learned it from watching people like YOU sacrifice for others every single day.

So be PROUD of the service members we honor today.

But also be PROUD of the fight and sacrifice YOU make.

Feeding the hungry. Serving the community. Running toward need.

That's the warrior's heart too.

And THAT'S what builds the kind of people who are willing to give everything for freedom. [PAUSE]

So what do we do with this?

1. REMEMBER

Say their names. Tell their stories. Don't let them be forgotten.

Every name on this plaque. Every soldier who didn't come home. Every family grieving today.

Remember them.

2. LIVE FULLY

They died so you could be free. Honor that by LIVING.

Don't waste the freedom they bought with their blood.

Enjoy your hotdogs. Drink your beer. Laugh with your family.

That's not dishonoring them. That's HONORING them.

Because that's why they served.

3. BE WILLING TO FIGHT FOR GOOD

Evil still exists. And good people still need to stand against it.

You might not be called to wear a uniform.

But you ARE called to stand for what's right.

In your home. In your workplace. In your community.

Don't look away from evil. Stand against it.

4. HONOR THEIR FAMILIES

The fallen gave everything. But their families are still here. Still grieving. Still carrying the weight.

Check on them. Remember them. Support them.

They sacrificed too.

5. FOLLOW JESUS

The One who gave EVERYTHING so we could be reconciled to God.

The One who laid down His life - not just for His friends, but for His enemies.

That's the Gospel.

That's the ultimate sacrifice.

And that's what makes sense of every other sacrifice we honor today. [PAUSE]

Kayla's on that plaque. These service members are on that plaque.

And their sacrifice points us to the cross.

Where Jesus said: "It is finished."

The price is paid. The gap is bridged. The way is open.

Greater love has no one than this. [LONG PAUSE]

That's what we honor today.

Sacrifice. Service. Love.

And ultimately - Jesus. [LONG PAUSE]

Let's pray.

Closing Prayer

And help us love like they loved - willing to give everything for others. And most of all, help us remember Jesus - the One who gave EVERYTHING so we could come home to You. In Jesus' name, Amen."*
